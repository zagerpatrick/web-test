Neutrophil Membrane Dynamics

Cell types across the tree of life maintain excess membrane. Fibroblasts, ameboid dictyostelium, early stage mammalian embryos, and nearly all immune cell types, organize portions of their plasma membrane into thin structures rich in membrane and poor in cytoplasm. While these structures have unique functions within any given cell type/organism, less is understood about how this organization of membrane also defines shape and mechanical state.

Despite the breadth of examples of membrane reservoirs across biology, their role in immune cells is understudied despite interest. Neutrophils, the first responder of the innate immune system, maintain excess membrane in folds and filopodia. The work herein aims to better understand the role of the neutrophil membrane reservoir in cell shape, cell shape change, and migration.
